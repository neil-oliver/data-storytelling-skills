# Part-to-whole charts

## Pie
- Use pie only for proportions of a whole, about five categories or fewer.
- Draw pies flat 2D, never tilted; default to bars for comparison.
- Avoid pies with many thin slices; switch to a sorted bar.
- Order pie and doughnut segments from largest to smallest.
- Avoid pie or doughnut when segments are similar in size and hard to compare.
- Give the focus slice a distinct color; render the rest as shades of one hue.
- Label small slices outside the pie to avoid crowding.

## Doughnut
- Use a doughnut to compare one segment against the whole, not segments to each other.
- Show the headline number in a doughnut's centre space.

## Waffle
- Use a waffle chart as a cleaner part-to-whole alternative to pie; each square is one percent.

## Stacked bar
- Use stacked bars to show a total alongside its parts.
- Use stacked bars over simple bars when the story is part-of-whole percentages.
- Order segments consistently; put the focus segment against the baseline.
- Only the bottom and top segments read reliably — place the comparison there.
- Give the bottom, most-important segment the strongest color.
- Limit stacked series to about ten categories before they get illegible.

## 100% stacked bar
- Use a 100% stacked bar to compare composition shares across categories.
- Avoid it when absolute totals, not shares, are the point.

## Treemap
- Use a treemap for part-to-whole across many nested categories; size blocks by share.
- Avoid treemaps with many tiny blocks; dense grids overwhelm readers.
- Prefer bars when categories are few and precision counts.

## Ternary
- Use a ternary chart when three components sum to a fixed whole (100%).
- Map each part to one vertex; read a value along the gridline parallel to the opposite side.

## Over time
- For composition over time, use a normalized stacked area — see [change-over-time](change-over-time.md).
